# Key Insights — Thamak Supply Chain Analytics

## Project Context

This case study uses simulated data to analyse a vanilla export supply chain affected by seasonal harvests, curing lead times, supplier performance and year-round customer demand.

All business names, transactions and financial figures are fictional.

## 1. Customer Fulfilment

- Total customer demand: 1,290.9 kg
- Fulfilled quantity: 985.7 kg
- Unfulfilled quantity: 305.2 kg
- Fulfilment rate: 76.36%

### Insight

The model records 305.2 kg of unmet opening demand before the first simulated curing batches become market-ready in July 2025.

This highlights the importance of aligning the start of procurement and curing activities with customer demand.

## 2. Inventory Management

- Closing inventory: 874.7 kg
- Highest monthly closing inventory: 904.8 kg in September 2026
- December 2026 closing inventory: 589.2 kg
- December 2026 inventory cover: approximately 5.33 months

### Insight

Inventory builds following seasonal harvest and curing cycles, then declines as customer orders are shipped.

The business needs to monitor inventory cover and schedule procurement early enough to accommodate curing lead times.

## 3. Profitability

- Shipped sales revenue: £103,750.79
- Estimated landed cost: £66,763.92
- Gross profit: £36,986.86
- Gross margin: 35.65%

### Insight

The simulated model indicates a positive gross margin after allocated procurement and recorded logistics costs.

Profitability should be reviewed across customer segments and product grades to identify where commercial performance is strongest.

## 4. Supplier Quality

- Batches tested: 40
- Batches passing defined project specifications: 24
- Batches failing: 16
- Overall CoA pass rate: 60%

### Insight

Quality performance varies across suppliers and batches.

The model supports a supplier scorecard that evaluates delivery reliability, purchase cost and simulated CoA results together.

The moisture and vanillin thresholds are project assumptions, not confirmed regulatory or customer requirements.

## 5. Customer Segments

Distributors account for approximately 52.4% of total demand and 212 kg of the model's unmet demand.

### Insight

Distributors represent the largest customer-segment exposure in the simulated demand model.

Demand planning and fulfilment reviews should consider customer segment, order size and the timing of available stock.

## 6. Recommended Business Actions

1. Plan procurement around seasonal harvests and curing lead times.
2. Monitor inventory cover against expected future demand.
3. Evaluate suppliers using cost, delivery reliability and quality.
4. Investigate the causes of unmet demand and distinguish opening backlog from later operational performance.
5. Compare gross margins across customer segments and product grades.
6. Investigate failed CoA results and review quality controls.

## Limitations

These findings are derived from simulated data. They demonstrate analytical methods and possible business decisions but do not represent verified commercial performance or validated forecasts.
